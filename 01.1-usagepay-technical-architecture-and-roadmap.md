## 1. Decision and falsifiable thesis

UsagePay is **evidence-grade metered settlement infrastructure**, built on Stellar MPP rather than a competing payment protocol. It receives external metering evidence from domain adapters, calculates tariff-based amounts, permits an authorized payer to approve cumulative channel commitments, and produces an independently checkable off-chain relationship between service evidence, signed financial commitments, and on-chain settlement.

**Primary hypothesis:** a reusable evidence and reconciliation bridge between metering machines and Stellar payment channels creates value beyond domain billing standards and the MPP SDK. **Commercial hypothesis, separate:** some fleets, CPOs, eMSPs or metered-service operators will pay for reliable managed channel operations. Neither proposition is proven by the standards alone.

**Working tagline:** *Infrastructure for the machine economy.* Messaging, use-case framing and claim guardrails live in [01.2 §0.1](docs/usagepay/01.2-usagepay-product-foundation.md#01-positioning-and-messaging-single-source-for-the-ui-readme-and-pitch). The tagline is aspirational; simulated signatures, unsigned interval readings and ordinary channel contracts cannot prove physical truth or cryptographically condition settlement on metrology. In precise materials prefer: **“Link measured service usage to authorized Stellar channel payments and verifiable settlement records.”** Machines consume; the fleet/operator is the contractual payer and controls signing authority.

## 2. Why this exists — and when it does not

EV charging standards already cover charger identity, transaction flow, signed metering transport, tariffs and roaming CDRs. MPP already covers payment challenges, channels, cumulative signed amounts and settlement. Do **not** claim innovation in those separate components. Stellar `@stellar/mpp` also ships watchers, on-chain state checks, dispute callbacks and fail-closed settlement behavior.

What remains as a **proposed application-layer seam**: `domain session -> measurement provenance -> tariff snapshot -> payer-authorized cumulative amount -> commitment reference -> settlement transaction -> independently replayable reconciliation`.

A direct OCPP/CSMS-to-MPP integration may be enough for some operators. If the same bridge cannot be reused across two genuinely different metering domains or operators do not see recurring value, narrow the project to a reference integration/open-source toolkit, or stop. Prior-art search found no public exact equivalent on Stellar in the reviewed sample; this is **not** proof of worldwide uniqueness.

## 3. Reference market and contracting boundary

- **Primary initial payer of record:** fleet operator (EV fleets, delivery, car-sharing). This is an initial persona hypothesis, not a universal EV contractual arrangement. A payer-controlled service owns private commitment keys and spending policy.
- **Service/payee:** charge-point operator (CPO), represented by its platform/CSMS and settlement endpoint. It owns metering ingestion and must preserve settlement evidence and react to `close_start`.
- **Secondary payer:** eMSP; can be the actual commercial intermediary in roaming. Validate the real contract/credit chain with an operator rather than generalizing.
- **Initial domain:** OCPP 2.x charging session, simulated equipment and OCMF-shaped signed readings. **Second-domain falsification:** signed energy meter over HTTP/MQTT; not a second EV-shaped DTO masquerading as a new standard.
- **Excluded:** consumer payment terminal and AFIR replacement, charger firmware, grid dispatch, OCPI roaming hub, discovery marketplace, certified metrology/PKI issuance and regulated lending.

| Channel voucher, verification, open/close/settlement | Stellar `@stellar/mpp` + one-way-channel | Experimental/unaudited channel contract; testnet only |
| Evidence bundle and reconciliation | UsagePay evidence core | Application-level association; not on-chain metering enforcement |
| Multi-tenant watcher, recovery, SLAs | Proposed managed service | SDK has primitives already; economic buyer unvalidated |

## 5. Evidence levels and verification limits

- **L0 — asserted:** values claimed by operator/simulator with no authenticated device origin.
- **L1 — authenticated transport/protocol:** integrity or origin asserted by an operator system; does **not** equal a signed metrology record.
- **L2 — signed meter reading:** signature and payload verified against a pinned meter public key. Add separate `certification_status` and `key_provenance`, since even L2 may be a simulated or non-certified meter.

An OCMF-shaped payload signed by our test key is **L2-simulated**, never a legally certified real meter. OCA guidance shows that signed *start and stop* values can be supplied without signed *intermediate* values; policy must support provisional intra-session accounting and final reconciliation with authoritative terminal readings. For tariff changes or per-interval legal requirements, evidence availability must be validated with the target hardware and jurisdiction.

**Allowed claims:** verify a known public key signed exact bytes; replay tariff mathematics; verify a financial commitment; locate and inspect on-chain settlement; detect mismatches. **Not allowed:** the blockchain proves actual kWh; settlement contract rejects false meters; all CPOs emit signed ticks; signatures automatically confer legal compliance; the whole flow is atomic.

## 6. Two separate proofs, not one atomic assertion

**Service evidence chain:** raw measurement + source/session IDs + interval + signer/key provenance + trust level + tariff snapshot + deterministic amount + stable canonical hash.

**Financial chain:** payer identity + channel id + domain/network + monotonically increasing cumulative amount + actual signed commitment + on-chain close/settle transaction.

**Binding record:** independently validates the mapping and timing between these two chains; it does **not** become a condition enforced by the current one-way-channel contract. Its signature/authorization model must prevent a payee from rewriting history after commitments have been signed.

**Hackathon P0 — authenticated evidence-to-payment link:** before E0 is finalized, the payer must separately sign an acknowledgement covering the canonical measurement-and-tariff evidence digest, domain session, network/channel, actual MPP commitment digest, approved cumulative amount and a replay-resistant nonce. The independent verifier must recompute the digest and verify **both** the native MPP financial signature **and** this payer acknowledgement; stored `verified: true` flags or matching JSON references alone are insufficient. The precise encoding and signing API belong in the engineering SPEC. This remains an **off-chain association**, not meter enforcement by the channel contract.

**No circular anchors:** pre-settlement record `E0` is immutable and may be anchored in a distinct transaction or protocol-supported commitment. After settlement, a final record `E1 = {E0_hash, settlement_tx_hash, settlement_outcome, verifier_version}` is appended and may receive a **separate later anchor**. `E0` cannot contain its own future settlement transaction hash. Mechanisms (transaction memo vs independent contract/event) are subject to SDK feasibility and cost; an anchor is not automatically a proof of measurement truth.

## 7. State, safety and concurrency invariants

1. The payer controls the commitment signing key. Server-proposed amounts must be checked against accepted meter evidence, tariff snapshot and payer-side budgets before the payer signs.
2. Monetary amounts use smallest integer units. Reading IDs are deduplicated; interval boundaries are half-open, explicit and replayable; no double charging on retries.
3. Multiple vehicles/sessions share a channel **only with** an independent, serializable channel-global cumulative counter plus per-session ledgers and a deterministic allocation/reconciliation rule.
4. An authorization is not a physical control command. “Pause” means *financial authorization stops*; charging hardware may continue until the CSMS/domain actually enforces a stop.
5. Persist the highest accepted signed commitment and enough evidence to restore it. Multi-instance production requires **linearizable compare-and-set**; `Store.memory()` is single-process reference only.
6. Watcher behavior (on dispute / `close_start`) and stale `settling` recovery are distinct from the already supplied SDK watcher primitive. Never clear an ambiguous `settling` flag without checking chain state.
7. A recipient can lose funds if it fails to settle within the contract's refund/closing window; timeout, network availability and key rotation are material risks. **Unaudited contract: testnet only.**
8. Ordinary public Stellar transactions reveal financial and counterparty metadata even if UsagePay publishes only hashes. No confidentiality claim for standard USDC channels.

## 8. Architecture — from minimal concept to realistic boundaries

### 8.1 Five-line picture (for a new engineer)

```text
Meter reads consumption -> UsagePay checks evidence and calculates cost
                    -> Fleet independently authorizes a cumulative amount
                    -> Stellar MPP carries the signed commitment
                    -> Channel settles on Testnet; verifier compares both histories
```

The **meter is not the wallet**. In the first demo, an EV fleet is the contractual payer, and a charging-point operator (CPO) is the payee. This is a chosen B2B scenario, not a claim that all charging markets contract this way.

### 8.2 System architecture (implementation target, not completed code)

```text
           DEVICE / DOMAIN TRUST                   FINANCIAL TRUST

  [Simulated OCPP 2.x charger]      [Independent signed energy meter]*
             | OCPP events / OCMF            | HTTP / MQTT sample
             v                               v
     [OCPP + OCMF adapter]              [MeterAdapter]*
             +---------------+---------------+
                             |
                    [Normalized readings]
                   raw bytes + signer + time
                             |
                  [Evidence verifier / L0-L2]
                             |
                [Deterministic tariff snapshot]
                             |
           [Proposed increment + per-session ledger]
                             |
              --------- TRUST BOUNDARY ----------
                             v
           [FLEET: payer-controlled policy & signer]
             budget / meter assurance / approval
                             |
                    signed cumulative amount
                             v
              [Stellar @stellar/mpp adapter]
                             |
        [Experimental one-way-channel / Testnet USDC]
           [E0: finalized evidence BEFORE channel close]
                             |
                close / settlement tx reference
                             |
        [E1: post-settlement tx record] -> [Independent Verify]

 * Second adapter: P1 only after first end-to-end path is stable.
 Sidecar only, P1: [recipient watcher] -> [chain inspection] -> [safe close].
 Future Product B: HA storage, on-call, alerts, reconciliation and SLAs.
```

**Boundary rules:** a payee may propose metered costs but may not impersonate the payer. UsagePay cannot infer certification from a signature. Ordinary Stellar USDC channels are publicly observable. Evidence records are off-chain; optional digests on-chain do not hide channel amounts. A policy refusal blocks **financial authorization**, not the physical charger's operation.

### 8.3 Detailed sequence — normal session and final verification

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant Meter as Simulated OCPP meter
    participant CPO as CPO / UsagePay intake
    participant Fleet as Fleet policy + signer
    participant SDK as Stellar MPP channel
    participant Chain as Stellar Testnet
    participant Verify as Independent verifier
    Meter->>CPO: TransactionEvent + reading + optional signature
    CPO->>CPO: Retain raw bytes; verify origin; tag L0/L1/L2
    CPO->>CPO: Replay pinned tariff; propose cumulative cost
    CPO->>Fleet: Evidence reference + amount + channel ID
    Fleet->>Fleet: Verify accepted input and hard budgets
    Fleet-->>CPO: Payer-signed MPP commitment + evidence acknowledgement
    CPO->>SDK: Verify/store monotonic commitment
    Note over Meter,CPO: Other increments repeat; interim readings may be unsigned
    CPO->>CPO: Finalize E0 before close; no future tx hash
    CPO->>SDK: Close / settle latest valid commitment
    SDK->>Chain: Channel settlement transaction

    Verify->>CPO: Read original evidence + E0/E1
    Verify->>Chain: Read independently confirmed settlement
    Verify-->>Fleet: Separate verdicts, discrepancies, limitations
```

The above sequence describes a proposed implementation. Actual SDK APIs, chain finality and exact contract transitions must be pinned and demonstrated in the engineering SPEC. The native channel contract **does not verify the meter evidence**.

### 8.4 Money and shared-channel example (illustrative only)

A sample session uses a fixed tariff of **0.40 USDC/kWh**; all numbers below are invented for tests, not operator tariffs. Working balances are shown in USDC for readability. In code, amounts must be converted with the **pinned asset's exact decimal scale** and processed in fixed-point integers.

| Accepted cumulative energy | New energy | Added cost | Payer-approved channel total | Evidence state |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0.25 kWh | 0.25 kWh | 0.10 USDC | 0.10 USDC | Interim, possibly provisional |
| 0.75 kWh | 0.50 kWh | 0.20 USDC | 0.30 USDC | Interim, possibly provisional |
| 1.00 kWh | 0.25 kWh | 0.10 USDC | 0.40 USDC | Final reconciliation only if signed terminal evidence available |

A signed end reading may disagree with intermediate telemetry; the system must flag a discrepancy and apply an explicit adjustment/dispute policy. The current prototype does **not** promise an automatic refund. For two fleet vehicles in **one** channel, the channel-global cumulative approved amount must equal the sum of allocations from distinct, idempotent per-session ledgers. The shared channel is P2, not the primary golden path.

**E0 must be immutable before chain settlement; E1 is appended only afterward.** Anchoring either hash is an independent optional chain operation; a closing transaction cannot simultaneously commit to a hash containing its own future transaction hash. A binding record or verifier does not cause the Stellar channel contract to enforce metering validity.

Example independent verification output:

```text
Meter payload signature:       PASS (against pinned SIMULATED key)
Device certification:          NOT AVAILABLE (not real certified hardware)
Tariff replay:                PASS (pinned version, fixed-point conversion)
Payer financial commitment:   PASS (actual verified MPP signature)
Service/commitment mapping:   PASS (independently verified payer-signed evidence acknowledgement)
On-chain settlement:          PASS (actual Testnet transaction inspected)
Private settlement:           NOT PROVIDED (ordinary public Stellar channel)
On-chain meter enforcement:   NOT PROVIDED (contract checks financial amount)
```

The MPP payment signature alone does not bind `raw_payload_sha256` to a particular service. The **separate payer acknowledgement is required for MVP P0**; a `PASS` result is permitted only after its signature and exact evidence-to-commitment scope are independently verified. Do not market the binding as atomic or tamper-proof merely because E0 contains hashes.

## 10. Roadmap after HackMeridian — two independent commercial tracks

**Permanent strategic distinction:** Product A (*Evidence Infrastructure*) and Product B (*Managed Settlement Operations*) **can complement each other, but are not the same commercial proposition**. A may succeed as a developer library/public good without recurring SaaS. B could support 24/7 operations, SLAs, recurring fees and enterprise contracts **only if actual Stellar channel adoption, operational feasibility and buyer willingness-to-pay justify it**. Their engineering may share a core; evaluate adoption, pricing, customers and unit economics separately.

### 10.1 Detailed roadmap (Hackathon through 90 days)

| Window | Product A — evidence/open-source | Product B — managed channel operations | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25–26 Oct (hackathon)** | Demonstrate exact bytes -> verification -> tariff -> payer commitment -> E0/E1 and Testnet settlement | SDK watcher only if P0 is stable | Working proof vs clearly narrowed fallback |
| **Days 1–14** | Publish a precise evidence format, test vectors and independent verifier; seek genuine OCMF samples; benchmark direct CSMS-to-MPP integration | Interview CPO/payee and payer about incident pain, collateral, SLAs and trust responsibilities | At least one independent operator / implementer signal; no PMF inference |
| **Days 15–45** | Validate a second genuinely different domain, compare adapter time/complexity; test independent source data and replay | Prototype durable CAS, chain-state reconciler for stale `settling`, alerting/exposure controls | Is shared core materially reusable and is ops pain recurrent? |
| **Days 46–90** | Public beta/reference library if users request it; assess SCF integration/public-good fit, community test-vector feedback | Scoped sandbox with willing operator; model custody, liabilities, FX/off-ramp, audits and gross margin before fee proposal | Track A Outcome A/B; Track B Outcome C *investigation* only if buyer signs on |

### 10.2 Longer-term direction — conditional, not commitments

1. **Track A:** production-quality adapter/test-vector conformance, independent verification, external contributors and reusability across domains. If direct integrations are sufficient, publish targeted examples instead of maintaining an unnecessary platform.
2. **Track B:** audited channel-usage stack, multi-tenant reliable service, key custody and delegated authority decisions, safe refunds/close, incident response, monitored collateral and contractual SLOs. **No production use with the experimental unaudited channel.**
3. **Integration option:** export service-to-settlement evidence for FlowCredit risk systems and VeilPay selective disclosure, but never make either a prerequisite for A or B.
4. **Commercial option:** explore grants, services or commercial subscriptions independently. Grants finance ecosystem R&D; they do not establish product-market fit.

### 10.3 Six-week A/B/C continuation and kill rules

| Real observation | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Target CSMS integration can directly handle evidence/payment reconciliation with trivial maintenance | **A — already sufficiently solved** | Publish reference adapter or stop standalone product |
| Independent developers use verifier/adapters but reject hosted recurring service | **B — reusable public good** | Maintain OSS; measure integration savings; consider eligible grant route |
| Fleet/CPO agrees to an actual sandbox pilot and shows recurring 24/7 channel exposure / settlement pain | **C — conditional product experiment** | Validate liability, privacy, fees, audited contract, security and SLA before pricing |

One pilot is **not** a demonstration of product-market fit. No particular grant amount, timeline for audits or channel-volume projection is guaranteed.

## 11. Design decisions deliberately left to the engineering SPEC

The strategy is finalized **without prematurely claiming implemented behavior**. The forthcoming SPEC must decide and test:

1. Exact pinned MPP SDK, one-way-channel code, asset decimals, channel state transitions and RPC client APIs.
2. **E0 integrity binding (MVP P0):** choose/test canonical encoding and the payer-side signing API for an independent acknowledgement covering evidence/tariff digest, session, network/channel, MPP commitment digest, cumulative amount and nonce; verify it independently of the native MPP signature. Key registry and replay/idempotency scope must be explicit; the native payment signature by itself does not bind OCMF.
3. **E0/E1 publication/anchoring:** if an anchor is included, use a distinct correctly ordered transaction/event; do not create a future-tx-hash circular dependency.
4. Identity mapping and least-privilege boundary across fleet signer, CPO payee, meter and public chain.
5. Final-only signed reading behavior, tariff rounding/version changes, provisional liability, overpayment/dispute semantics.
6. Stable aggregate channel book vs per-session ledger, CAS durability, race recovery and watch/close safety if features are implemented.
7. Independent verification with explicit `PASS / FAIL / NOT_AVAILABLE`, finality/reorg handling, logs/privacy retention and demo reproducibility.
8. Real operator requirements and measured ROI remain discovery, not invented interface constraints.

Do **not** begin a second strategy document to answer these: they are engineering specification questions.
