# 01.3 — UsagePay: Viability, Evidence and Falsification Gates

**Document version:** 1.1.0 · **Strategy reviewed:** 2026-10-25 · **HackMeridian begins:** Sunday, 2026-10-25 · **Status:** Independent viability assessment; 01.1 owns build priorities · **Authority:** 01.3 for falsification / GO gates; 01.1 for implementation

## 0. Purpose: do not turn hackathon confidence into market certainty

Judge the **three different bets** separately: a working Genesis prototype, a useful Stellar OSS integration, and a paid settlement service. 

Labels: **VERIFIED PUBLIC** means a primary source/code supports the exact statement; **INFERENCE** is our interpretation of public material; **HYPOTHESIS** lacks target-market validation; **OPEN RISK** is a possible blocker; **KILL** means change course.

## 2. A/B/C result and conditions

- **Outcome A — sufficiently solved by direct integrations:** clearly true for some consumer EV and digital pay-per-request cases. For target CPO/fleet B2B, still a falsification possibility.
- **Outcome B — reusable but fragmented:** **current research-supported working position**, not a validated ecosystem product. One working cross-source verifier and a useful integration reference are reasonable Genesis outputs.
- **Outcome C — independent commercial service:** **not validated**. Requires recurring pain, an authorized buyer, plausible savings or service value, operational funding/treasury feasibility and a customer-backed pilot.

Therefore: **Proceed to HackMeridian Genesis as an evidence-layer prototype; do not claim a commercial GO or originality-first verdict.** Stop/redesign the channel portion if the pre-event SDK test fails rather than improvising a misleading demo.

## 3. Judges' five high-risk questions

### 3.1 “Isn't this just MPP?”

MPP moves and authorizes funds. UsagePay **proposes** to correlate external signed/source-authenticated measurement, tariff version, payer policy, signed commitment, and settlement into an auditable record. It does not modify the contract's authorization semantics. Existing systems may still solve enough of this; show incremental evidence test cases to justify the bridge.

### 3.2 “Why blockchain instead of an invoice or Stripe?”

The MVP demonstrates a technically different settlement primitive: repeated off-chain payer-signed cumulative authorizations and eventual Stellar on-chain settlement. It does **not** prove lower end-to-end cost, better B2B buyer acceptance or improved regulatory outcomes. Compare invoices, prefunding, treasury and FX in the pilot.

### 3.3 “Is the consumption really provable?”

Not completely. An OCMF-shaped simulator record cryptographically demonstrates signature verification against a simulator key. Real regulatory assurance requires device key provenance, certification/calibration and appropriate signed-reading availability. Display this limitation in the README and Verify page. No meter data is validated by the channel contract.

### 3.4 “Didn't the SDK already ship a watcher?”

Yes. A **service** can still package monitored channels, persistent monotonic ledgers, multi-tenant CAS, incident response, alerting, automated safe close and reconciliation. That managed service is **unvalidated commercially** and distinct from the developer evidence library.

### 3.5 “Why would a fleet reveal settlement volume and lock USDC?”

We do not know whether it would. Evidence hashes off-chain do not hide public channel value or relationships. Working capital locked per fleet–operator pair, settlement risk, off-ramp and confidentiality could defeat the proposition. These questions are reasons to interview, not issues the hackathon resolves.

## 4. Assessment gates (not a second implementation roadmap)

- **Technical validity gate:** the pinned upstream channel lifecycle must actually work on Stellar Testnet; a Charge-only fallback demonstrates a narrower capability and must be disclosed.
- **Evidence-integrity gate:** the team must show that mutating a raw reading or supplying an unknown key fails verification **and prevents any new payer authorization**. This is a **P0** acceptance test in 01.1, not an optional nice-to-have.
- **Assurance gate:** terminal-only signed records are identified honestly; simulated keys never imply certified hardware.
- **Commercial gate:** evaluate direct CSMS-to-MPP alternatives, payer/payee adoption interest, channel treasury and public-ledger privacy before claiming a standalone service.

**The one authoritative sprint backlog, pre-event deadline and demonstration timings are in [01.1 §9](../../01.1-usagepay-technical-architecture-and-roadmap.md).** This document evaluates outcomes, not task ordering.

## 5. Evaluation rubric (internal estimate, not official judge score)

| Criterion | Target evidence | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Problem clarity | Operator-to-fleet evidence mismatch and documented prior art | “Yet another pay-per-kWh app” |
| Stellar depth | Real one-way-channel lifecycle and signed cumulative commitments | Only simulated payment or vague wallet click |
| Technical correctness | Deterministic amount, independent verifier, no atomicity claim | “Blockchain verifies kWh” misinformation |
| Differentiation | Evidence before/after settlement with discrepancy test | Mere OCPP JSON sent to MPP endpoint |
| Reusability | One clean meter adapter interface, ideally second-domain spike | EV-only hard-coded accounting masquerades as horizontal |
| Honest risk disclosure | Unaudited channel, end-only signed readings, privacy, money lockup | Security or compliance overclaim |
| Continuation | Two product tracks with external pilot gates | Unsubstantiated SaaS ARR/SCF promises |

No arbitrary internal 31/40 score should be presented as an official Genesis evaluation.

## 6. Six-week post-event gates

| Observation | Recommended classification / next step |
|---|---|
| Verified customer signs sandbox pilot for evidence and real settlement | Test Outcome C; build operational/economic experiment |
| Useful evidence tool, no willingness for on-chain settlement | Outcome B, maybe fiat-linked evidence anchor; rethink need for MPP |
| Ecosystem asks for SDK tests/adapters but not service | Outcome B public-good/tooling grant application, not SaaS |
| Operators show direct CSMS→MPP is trivial and sufficient | Outcome A, publish integration/reference and stop standalone claims |
| Stablecoin privacy/collateral/FX destroys operator ROI | Narrow or pivot; do not promise eventual confidential tokens will solve all |
| Neither operators nor ecosystem stakeholders adopt it | Stop and reuse software/knowledge in other project |

A single pilot shows feasibility and interest, **not product–market fit**. No mainnet pilot until contract security, custody, regulatory and operational obligations are addressed.

## 7. Principal risks (register)

1. **Product:** assumed fleet/eMSP payer relationship may be wrong. Verify actual cash and invoice flow.
2. **Evidence:** signed intermediates may be unavailable; L2 signature from non-certified test key cannot be sold as Eichrecht-grade.
3. **Cryptographic binding:** external evidence is not in the on-chain commitment; correlation is independent application proof, not enforced payment condition.
4. **Anchor correctness:** E0 prior-to-close and E1 post-close; self-reference invalid. Pin published verifier version, hashes, replay formats.
5. **Ops:** SDF has watch primitives, but multi-tenant HA, CAS, stale settling, alerting and loss exposure are hard and untested.
6. **Economics:** prefund working capital and fees/FX/taxes/regulation can dominate low on-chain fees.
7. **Privacy:** off-chain evidence still leaves public financial metadata on Stellar.
8. **Prior art:** OCPP/OCPI and ROZO/Paykit/StellarPay overlap; broad first-mover claims would be false or unsubstantiated.
9. **Supply constraints:** one small team and 27 elapsed event hours; no time to safely invent new cryptography.
