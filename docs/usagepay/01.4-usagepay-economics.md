# 01.4 — UsagePay: Economics, Commercial Models and Ecosystem Value

**Document version:** 1.1.0 · **Strategy reviewed:** 2026-10-25 · **HackMeridian begins:** Sunday, 2026-10-25 · **Status:** Economic assumptions and sensitivity tests; not a product roadmap · **Authority:** 01.4 for economics; 01.1 for milestone dates and feature priorities

## 0. Economic question and key decision

If the technical evidence layer works, **who has recurring costs large enough to pay for it**, and could the Stellar ecosystem fund it even without an immediate commercial business?

**Critical distinction to preserve in every roadmap and pitch:**

> **Product A — Evidence Infrastructure:** an open-source library, adapters, evidence schemas, validation/test vectors and verifier tools. It may provide substantial developer/public-good value independently of revenue.
>
> **Product B — Managed Settlement Operations:** hosted channel watchers, monitoring, incident response, safe close, ledger reconciliation, alerts and SLAs. It may justify service contracts and recurring fees **if** operators adopt Stellar channels and value outsourcing the risk.

They can complement each other, but **they are not the same commercial proposition**. UsagePay can succeed as A and fail as B; a good A codebase does not constitute willingness-to-pay evidence for B.

## 1. Who pays and for what

| Economic actor | Possible cost avoided | Potential A value | Potential B value | Evidence today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fleet operator (contractual payer) | Invoice review, per-vehicle spend controls, disputes | Replayable consumption-to-commitment attribution | Policy/ops integration, availability | **Hypothesis:** no direct willingness-to-pay |
| CPO / service operator (payee) | Settlement lag, partner integration, missed channel closes | Evidence export and independent verification | 24/7 monitor, auto-settle, controlled liability | **Hypothesis:** buyer not interviewed |
| eMSP/roaming intermediary | CDR correlation and disputes | Standard integration/reference | Commercial operations / clearing | Existing hubs may already suffice |
| Stellar / developers | Missing test vectors and integration examples | OSS ecosystem adoption / SCF eligibility | Reduced channel operations integration burden | Public ecosystem investment != customer demand |
| Capital provider (later) | Low-trust revenue telemetry | Verified evidence inputs for underwriting | None necessarily | Credit-usefulness unmeasured |

## 2. Existing cost pools — do not conflate

**EV roaming hub fees:** public fee examples indicate per-session costs can exist. That does **not** mean a Stellar channel can remove all hub value; hubs also provide authentication, authorization, routing, liability and interoperability.

**Card processing fees:** often payer/provider-specific, particularly for ad hoc retail. A B2B fleet–CPO proposal generally **must not use avoided consumer card fees as its primary revenue case**.

**Integration/reconciliation labor:** perhaps the strongest buyer-side proposition, but no measured baseline has been supplied. Need real engineering and financial operations data.

**Settlement delay/credit:** instant or progressive authorized commitments can reduce exposure for some payees, but channel prefunding ties up **payer working capital** and moves risk rather than magically eliminates it. Refund windows, custody and off-ramp may make it worse.

**Confidentiality:** publicly observable balances, addresses and settlement amounts can impose economic or adoption costs for B2B operators. Hashing off-chain evidence does not remove this. Confidential-token readiness and compatibility are separate unknowns.

## 3. All-in customer ROI model

Let:

- `S` = settlement sessions per month; `N` = active payer–payee channels; `V` = monthly GMV (for analysis only).
- `H` = avoidable fee *actually replaced* per session, not gross hub fee.
- `R` = avoidable reconciliation/dispute operating cost per session.
- `I` = monthly integration/maintenance savings attributable to the new core.
- `F` = monthly UsagePay managed-service fee (if bought).
- `O` = managed operations not covered by provider; `X` = off-ramp, FX, treasury, legal/compliance overhead.
- `K` = economic cost of locked channel deposits; `L` = expected channel operational/security losses.

`Net monthly customer benefit = S × (H + R) + I − F − O − X − K − L`.

**No customer ROI claim** before each component is measured in a fleet/CPO setting. Avoid mixing fee-pool gross values with removable cost. Cash-flow benefit to payee and collateral cost to payer must be assessed **on opposite balance sheets**.

## 4. Product A economics: reference tooling / open-source

Possible sustainability sources: SCF integration/public-good grants if eligible and awarded, paid support, enterprise adapters, sponsorship, professional services, industry partners. These are **strategic options**, not awarded funding.

Success metrics are not ARR first: independent verifier installations; external contributors; test vector adoption; protocols supported without core rewrites; correctness defects uncovered; SDK/standards integrations; time-to-integrate reduced in a reproducible benchmark.

Failure signal: each vertical requires a wholly bespoke implementation and direct CSMS-to-MPP integration is simpler; maintainers then publish narrow samples rather than marketing a generic platform.

## 5. Product B economics: managed operated channels

Potential pricing variables (illustrative only): `active_channel_month`, `settled_session`, enterprise support/SLA tier, or a small platform fee plus cost pass-through. Percent-of-GMV fees are especially risky if margins are thin and all-in off-ramp fees dominate.

**Unit economics illustration — purely hypothetical:**

| Scenario | Paying active channels | Fee/channel/month | Illustrative monthly revenue | What it ignores |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Early pilot | 10 | €20 | €200 | 24/7 support, custody, audit, compliance |
| Small operator base | 100 | €20 | €2,000 | On-call, incident losses and churn |
| Significant network adoption | 1,000 | €20 | €20,000 | Sales, infra, multi-tenancy, regulated dependencies |

These are mathematical sensitivity cases, **not** forecasted customers, feasible prices or profit. At 10 channels, availability requirements likely make managed-service economics unattractive absent broader service revenue. Make a gross-margin model after pilot data.

**Cost of service:** RPC, state database with linearizable CAS, monitoring, alerting, multi-region failover, support, incident response, key custody/HSM design, audits, chain volatility, counterparty risk and catastrophic refund-window mistakes. Hosting a watcher does not guarantee funds are safe.

## 6. Prefunding and channels: the hidden cost

One channel per **fleet–operator pair** may be more capital-efficient than one per vehicle, but demands shared, serialized accounting. For N counterparties each requiring `D_i` prefund balance, `locked liquidity = Σ D_i`. Cost of capital at annual fraction `r` is approximately `monthly K = (Σ D_i × r)/12` under constant balances, excluding FX and liquidity shocks.

Illustration: 20 relationships × €500 average prefund × 8% annual capital cost -> ~€66.67/month **pure opportunity cost**. Figures are hypothetical. This expense falls on the funding party; recipient might gain credit-risk protection. The integration cannot be sold as free liquidity or fee-free payments.

## 7. Two-stage commercial discovery

**Stage A:** prove some technical stakeholders need independent evidence ingestion and standardized verifier/test vectors. Interview EV and non-EV teams; benchmark adapter effort against direct integration.

**Stage B:** separately interview potential buyers of 24/7 channel operations and model incident exposure: what do they do today, what SLO is needed, what happens if recipient misses `close_start`, what maximum loss will they tolerate, who retains keys, how much will they pay?

No answer to A alone answers B. No private usage data should be invented in financial projections.

## 8. Stellar/SDF benefit and grant thesis

Stellar may gain reference implementations, verified metering evidence linked to asset settlement, broader integrations and increased usage. Actual blockchain-fee revenue may remain tiny because MPP channels aggregate payments by design. It is **incorrect** to equate GMV, prefunded USDC, protocol fees, SaaS revenue and SDF benefit.

A grant is venture/public-good financing subject to program criteria and review, **not a business model or proof of product-market fit**. Prior funded machine-payments projects show ecosystem interest, not exclusivity or guaranteed SCF approval.

## 9. Commercial validation inputs for the single roadmap

The **complete and only authoritative UsagePay timeline** appears in [01.1 §10](../../01.1-usagepay-technical-architecture-and-roadmap.md). The financial workstreams that must be evaluated during those milestones are listed here without a conflicting schedule:

- **Product A:** integration labor avoided; test-vector/verifier adoption; cross-domain core reuse; support/grant eligibility without assuming recurring revenue.
- **Product B:** who suffers channel incident losses; required uptime/on-call staffing; safe close and CAS costs; fee willingness; locked deposits, off-ramp/FX, liability, custody and incident risk.
- **Shared but separate:** public ledger disclosure and payer policy authority are technical blockers; their financial impact may differ by customer. An A adopter is not evidence of a B buyer.

## 10. Independent success/kill metrics

**A success:** 2 independent external integrations or one domain-specific operator independently validates use of evidence verifier (numeric target as a planning heuristic, not an industry benchmark); traceable reduction in integration/incident reconciliation effort. **B success:** one paid-intent managed pilot, credible SLA boundaries and modeled positive gross margin at realistic utilization. **C product conclusion:** only when a buyer requests the operated component rather than just the free library.

**Kill independent product:** direct alternatives are trivial to implement or maintain; per-channel capital/treasury/compliance burden exceeds measurable value; no payer/payee wants Stellar settlement. **Keep public-good outcome:** strong external library adoption but no managed demand.
